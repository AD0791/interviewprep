# Dual-track positioning and the pitch

*You told the group you would consider a data analyst role or a developer role. That was the right answer to give an agency. This module makes it work instead of costing you.*

---

## 1. The problem this module solves

Imagine two candidates in front of the same recruiter.

The first says: *"I'm open to a data analyst role or a developer role."* The recruiter writes down "flexible" and, without meaning anything by it, files the candidate as generalist. Generalist in a VA placement pool is a category with a price attached, and it is not the price you want.

The second says: *"I'm a data analyst who builds the systems as well as the reports — so I fit analyst requisitions and I fit backend requisitions, and honestly the interesting ones are where a client needs both."* The recruiter writes down "analyst + engineering, senior." Same flexibility. Completely different filing.

Nothing about the underlying facts changed. What changed is whether the breadth was presented as **indecision** or as **range**. Indecision reads as "will take anything." Range reads as "expensive, and worth it."

The trap is real and specific to this industry. Placement agencies match candidates to open requisitions, and the fastest match is often the lowest-skilled one that fits. A technically strong candidate who sounds undecided gets slotted into general virtual-assistant work at a general virtual-assistant rate, and then spends two years explaining why their title does not match their skills. The defence is not to narrow your options — it is to state a **level** even while staying open on the **title**.

---

## 2. The rule: dual for the recruiter, single for the client

This is the whole module in one line, and it resolves the tension cleanly.

**With the recruiter (stage two), breadth is an asset.** They are scanning a list of open requisitions looking for a fit. A candidate who fits two categories is twice as likely to be matched this month, and throughput is what their business runs on. Give them the breadth openly.

**With the client (stage three), breadth is a liability.** The client has exactly one job with one job description and a budget line attached to it. Walking in and offering two identities makes you look unfocused and invites them to anchor you at the lower of the two rates. Once you know the requisition, you are the thing the requisition asks for, and the other half of your background becomes a *differentiator* rather than an alternative.

Concretely: if you are sent to an analyst role, you are a data analyst — and the engineering background is why you can also keep the pipeline alive, which is a bonus nobody else offers. If you are sent to a backend role, you are a backend developer — and the analytics background is why you understand what the data is *for*, which most backend developers do not.

Same facts. Different foreground.

---

## 3. The sentence that protects your rate

If you say only "I'm open to either," you have given the recruiter freedom on title *and* on level, and level is where the money is. Bind one and release the other.

> **"I'm open on the title, not on the level. Analyst or developer — both are technical roles, and I'd expect to be matched accordingly."**

Have this ready. Use it the first time flexibility comes up, said lightly, not defensively. It takes four seconds and it is probably worth several hundred dollars a month.

The corollary is a category you should decline explicitly and warmly, because otherwise silence will be read as consent:

> "One thing worth saying plainly so we don't waste anyone's time — I'm not looking for general virtual assistant or admin work. My background is technical and that's where I'd add value for a client."

Say it once, pleasantly, and move on. A recruiter would far rather know this at stage two than after a client interview goes wrong.

---

## 4. The unified profile

Here is the identity that spans both tracks without splitting into two. It is true, it is provable from your resume, and it is one person rather than two.

**You are a technical data professional who has worked both ends of the data stack — building the pipelines and the applications underneath, and the analysis and dashboards on top.**

The evidence, straight from the resume, and note how it lands on both sides at once:

| Engineering side | Analysis side |
|---|---|
| FastAPI REST services with JWT, repository pattern (Tekkod) | Power BI dashboards for weekly KPI monitoring (Caris) |
| Apache Beam ETL, MongoDB → BigQuery on GCP (Tekkod) | mWater and Power BI dashboards for stakeholder reporting (HANWASH) |
| React Native modernization, 0.65 → 0.74, New Architecture (Tekkod) | Looker Studio dashboards with search and filtering (Tekkod) |
| Python jobs on AWS, Docker, CI/CD | Python/SQL automated reporting, 40% less manual processing (Caris) |
| Query optimisation: 36 seconds to under 0.5 | Indicator frameworks, ITTs, KPI definitions across programmes |
| OAuth2/JWT RBAC, unit testing (CassionSoft/UNOPS) | Data quality and reconciliation protocols on MySQL (Caris) |

Read the two columns and notice they are not two careers. They are the same person following data from where it is generated to where a decision gets made. That framing is what makes "analyst or developer" sound like a coherent professional rather than someone hedging.

---

## 5. The 90-second dual-track pitch

Read it aloud five times. Do not memorise the words — memorised text sounds memorised, and a recruiter listening for English fluency will hear recitation immediately. Learn the five beats: **what I am · the engineering proof · the analysis proof · the range, bounded by level · why remote/why now.**

> "I'm a technical data professional with about five years of experience, and the short version of my background is that I've worked both ends of the data stack rather than just one.
>
> On the engineering side, I'm currently a software engineer at Tekkod — I led a React Native modernization project this year, and before that I built the backend and the data pipelines: FastAPI services, and an Apache Beam pipeline moving MongoDB data into BigQuery on Google Cloud. On the analysis side, I've owned reporting for multi-site programmes — defining the indicators, writing the SQL, and shipping the dashboards that leadership actually made decisions from, mostly in Power BI and Looker Studio.
>
> So when you ask what I'm looking for: I fit data analyst roles and I fit backend or fullstack developer roles, and the honest answer is that the ones I do best are where a client needs both — someone who can build the pipeline *and* tell them what the numbers mean. I'm open on the title; I'd just want it to be a technical role at the right level.
>
> And this way of working isn't new for me. Effectively all of my work for the last five years has been remote with distributed teams, including a couple of years with a US client through Upwork. I'm in Haiti, so I'm on Eastern time — full overlap with a US working day."

Five things that pitch does on purpose. It opens with a category, not a hedge. It gives concrete named proof on both sides instead of adjectives. It turns the dual track into a *stated preference for the intersection*, which is the strongest possible version of flexibility. It binds level while releasing title. And it closes on remote fit and timezone, which for a company built entirely on remote placement is the last thing you want left ringing.

**Time it.** If it runs past 100 seconds, cut the middle paragraph rather than the last one.

## 6. The follow-ups you will get, and the answers

### "Which do you actually prefer?"

Do not say "either, I don't mind." Give a preference with a reason, then keep the door open.

> "If I'm choosing, analytics — I like being close to the decision, and the part of the work I enjoy most is when someone changes what they're doing because of a number I put in front of them. But I say that as someone who'd rather build the thing than just consume it, so a backend role where I own the data side is very much in scope too."

That answer gives them something to file while forfeiting nothing.

### "Why are your job titles all over the place?"

This is the CV coherence question and you already have the material for it — the overlapping-dates map in [`first_step/04 §5`](../first_step/04_positioning_and_mock_interview.md). The short spoken version:

> "Several of those overlap because they were consulting engagements rather than full-time posts — that's normal in the Haitian market, where organisations contract specialists per project. Caris was my main programme engagement while Tekkod and HANWASH ran as parallel consulting contracts. The through-line is the same in all of them: data systems, and the software around them."

Say it once, plainly, and move on. Over-explaining is what turns a non-issue into an issue.

### "You've mostly worked in the humanitarian and NGO sector — this is commercial."

Expect this one. It is the real objection and it lands harder in a placement context, because the client is a US business.

> "That's fair, and it's the reason I'm looking at this route. What I'd push back on gently is the assumption that the work is different — a donor-funded programme is measured harder than most commercial teams I've seen, because the numbers are externally audited and you don't get to revise them. So the discipline transfers: defining a metric once so it means the same thing everywhere, reconciling systems that disagree, and building reporting people trust enough to act on. The vocabulary is different and I've been deliberately closing that gap. The rigour is the part that's already there."

`first_step/06_sales_lens_and_objections.md` has the longer treatment if this becomes a theme at stage three.

### "Do you have Tableau?"

You are working on it via YouTube right now, so answer honestly with a transfer argument attached. Never say "I'm a fast learner" on its own.

> "Not in production — my dashboard work has been Power BI, where I'd call myself expert, plus Looker Studio. I've been working through Tableau over the last couple of weeks and the concepts map closely; a FIXED LOD expression is doing the same job as CALCULATE with ALLEXCEPT in DAX, which is what I already think in. The interface is the real learning curve, not the ideas, and I'd expect to be productive in it within a couple of weeks. [If the link exists:] I've built a dashboard on a practice dataset — happy to send you the link."

Finish the Tableau Public link. With it, that answer stops being a concession and becomes evidence.

---

## 7. Two sentences to never say

**"I'll take whatever you have."** It ends the level conversation permanently and there is no recovering the anchor afterwards.

**"I'm still figuring out what I want to do."** True of almost everyone and fatal in a fifteen-minute screen. If you feel it, say "I'm open on the title" instead — same honesty, entirely different signal.
