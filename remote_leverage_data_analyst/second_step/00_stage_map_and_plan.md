## 2. What actually happens between stages

Placement agencies run administrative work in parallel with interviews, and candidates who are not expecting it lose days. Expect some or all of the following between now and stage three:

**A recorded video introduction or written profile.** They need something to send clients. It is often a short async recording — one to three minutes answering "introduce yourself and your experience." If you get one, use the pitch from [module 03](03_dual_track_positioning.md). Record it more than once. This artefact will be seen by more people than any live call you do.

**An English assessment.** Possibly live during stage two, possibly an async platform test with recorded speaking prompts. Their entire product proposition is English-speaking talent, so this is a hard gate.

**A cognitive or personality assessment.** CCAT-style aptitude tests are common in this industry, as are DISC-type personality inventories. You already have CCAT primer material from the BairesDev preparation in `../../03_mock_interviews_and_scenarios/` — files 05–08. Reuse it rather than rebuilding.

**Background and reference checks.** Have two or three referees warned and ready, with current email addresses. Tekkod and HANWASH are your strongest because both are remote engagements — a referee who can say "he worked remotely for us for years and delivered" answers the whole of [module 02](02_remote_work_evidence.md) for you.

**A technical assessment or take-home.** Less likely before stage three, but possible if a specific client demands it. This is where the Power BI and Tableau work pays off.

**Documents.** Government ID, sometimes proof of address, sometimes a signed candidate agreement. Read anything you sign — see [module 04 §5](04_rate_and_engagement_terms.md).

None of this is unusual. The point of listing it is that if you are waiting passively for "the next interview," you will be caught flat-footed by an assessment link with a 48-hour expiry.

## 3. What stage two screens for, in priority order

The recruiter's job is not to find the best analyst. It is to avoid a failed placement — because on a one-time flat fee model, a candidate who washes out during the guarantee period means doing the work twice for one payment. Every question they ask traces back to that.

**First, deliverability.** Can you actually do the work from where you are, on their clients' hours, without the connection dropping? This is your largest exposure and [module 02](02_remote_work_evidence.md) is the longest module in this folder for that reason.

**Second, communication.** English fluency, but also responsiveness, clarity, and whether you volunteer information or have to be pulled. A client who has to chase a remote contractor for updates fires them, and the recruiter knows it.

**Third, matchability.** What roles can they slot you into, at what level, at what rate. This is where your "analyst or developer" answer either widens your options or dilutes your price.

**Fourth, stability and motive.** Why you are looking, whether you will still be there in six months, whether you are interviewing elsewhere. Answer honestly and without complaint about anyone.

**Fifth — and much lower than you expect — technical depth.** A recruiter at a placement agency is usually not technical. They will take your claims at face value and let the client verify them. Do not perform depth at a recruiter; be clear, concrete and brief instead.

## 4. What stage three will be, so you can plan for it

You cannot prepare for stage three properly until you know the requisition, and getting that information is an explicit goal of stage two ([module 05 §7](05_question_bank.md)). What you can predict is the shape.

If the requisition is **analyst**, expect live SQL or a take-home, questions about a BI tool by name, and business-metric reasoning about the client's own funnel. `first_step/` modules 01, 02, 03, 05 and the question bank are written for exactly this and become your primary material overnight.

If it is **developer**, expect Python or JavaScript, a code exercise, and questions about how you work in someone else's codebase. Your Tekkod React Native modernization and the FastAPI/SQLAlchemy work are the spine of that story, and the BairesDev prep material in `../../` covers the stack.

Two questions come regardless of track. *Can you work without supervision?* — have a concrete story about a period where nobody was checking on you and you shipped anyway. And *why an agency?* — answer without apology; the honest version is that it gives you access to US clients you would not reach directly from Haiti, and that is a perfectly good reason.

## 5. The plan

You do not have a fixed date yet, so this is written as a sequence rather than a calendar. Compress it if the invitation arrives fast; the first block is the one you cannot skip.

**Block one — two hours, tonight or tomorrow.** Fill in [module 02](02_remote_work_evidence.md) with your real numbers. Run the speed test, check what your inverter or generator actually gives you, count the hours. This is the only module that requires data you do not already have, so it has to go first. While you are there, warn your referees.

**Block two — ninety minutes.** [Module 01](01_the_placement_model.md) and [module 03](03_dual_track_positioning.md). Read the dual-track pitch aloud five times. Do not memorise it — learn the four beats. Then record yourself saying it once and listen back, because that is what the video introduction will be.

**Block three — one hour.** [Module 04](04_rate_and_engagement_terms.md). Decide your floor and write the number down. Not a range you will improvise on the call — one number, decided while calm. Rehearse the deflection so it is automatic.

**Block four — ninety minutes, aloud.** [Module 05](05_question_bank.md). Work it as a quiz: read the question, answer out loud in English, then read the written answer and note the gap. Sitting and reading the answers feels productive and teaches you almost nothing.

**Block five — thirty minutes, the day of the call.** Re-read [module 02](02_remote_work_evidence.md) and the numbers in [module 04](04_rate_and_engagement_terms.md). Say the pitch twice. Then close everything and open only [99](99_during_call_one_pager.md).

**Standing, in parallel.** Your Power BI and Tableau YouTube work. Twenty minutes of spoken English every day, out loud, ideally reading something technical so the vocabulary comes with it.
