# Question bank — the recruiter one-on-one

*Work this as a quiz. Read the question, cover the answer, say yours out loud in English, then compare. Reading the answers silently feels productive and teaches you very little — recognition is not retrieval.*

---

## How to use this

Forty-one questions in seven groups, plus the six you must ask them. Every answer is written the way it should be **spoken**, not the way it would be written — so if it sounds slightly loose on the page, that is deliberate.

Answers marked **★** are the ones that decide the call. If you only rehearse five things, rehearse those.

For the two long-form set pieces — the 90-second pitch and the remote-work answer — the canonical versions live in [module 03 §5](03_dual_track_positioning.md) and [module 02 §4](02_remote_work_evidence.md). They are cross-referenced here rather than duplicated.

## Group 1 — Opening and motivation

**Q1 ★ Tell me about yourself.**
The 90-second dual-track pitch, [module 03 §5](03_dual_track_positioning.md). Five beats: what I am, engineering proof, analysis proof, range bounded by level, remote fit and timezone. Time it — past 100 seconds you are losing them.

**Q2. Why did you apply to Remote Leverage?**
> "Two reasons, and the first one is practical. I'm in Haiti, and the work I want — technical data work with US or international teams — is hard to reach directly from here. An agency that specialises in placing remote talent with US companies solves that access problem in a way that cold applications don't. The second is that I've already been working this way for five years, so this isn't me trying remote work; it's me trying to do more of what I already do."

Honest, unembarrassed about the access motive, and it flatters their actual value proposition rather than a generic one.

**Q3. What do you know about us?**
> "You place English-speaking remote talent from Latin America and the Philippines with US businesses, and the model that makes you different is the one-time flat fee — the client pays the placement fee once and then pays the person directly, rather than an ongoing margin through an agency. Which for the candidate means a direct relationship with the client, and for the client means the cost doesn't compound. I also saw the review scores are strong, around 4.9 on Glassdoor."

Knowing the fee model by name is a differentiator. Almost no candidate will have registered it.

**Q4. What are you looking for in your next role?**
> "A technical role — analyst or backend — with a team where I own something end to end rather than being handed tickets. The work I do best is where I can go from the data collection through to what the numbers mean, and I'd like the next role to have that shape."

**Q5. Why are you leaving your current role / why now?**
Answer forward, never backward. No complaints about anyone, ever — a recruiter hearing a candidate criticise a past employer immediately imagines you doing it about their client.
> "Nothing's wrong where I am — Tekkod's a good team and I'm mid-project. It's more that I want to move fully into the data side rather than splitting between that and application work, and I want to be doing it with US-based clients. That's what I'm looking for rather than what I'm running from."

**Q6. Are you interviewing anywhere else?**
> "I've got one or two other conversations going, nothing at offer stage. I'd rather be straight about that than pretend — but this is the process I'm most interested in because of the type of work involved."

Mild, honest, creates gentle urgency. Do not invent an offer you do not have; it is checkable more often than people think.

**Q7. Where do you see yourself in a few years?**
> "Doing the same category of work at a bigger scale — owning the data function for a company rather than a programme, and staying technical rather than moving into pure management. I don't have a title in mind. I do want to keep building things."

## Group 2 — Remote work and reliability

This group is the point of the call. Full treatment in [module 02](02_remote_work_evidence.md).

**Q8 ★ Do you have experience working remotely?**
[Module 02 §4](02_remote_work_evidence.md), the long answer. Five years, Tekkod and HANWASH named, infrastructure numbers volunteered unprompted, timezone advantage stated.

**Q9 ★ Describe your working setup.**
Speeds, backup connection, battery hours, dedicated workspace, headset. Real numbers. See the table in [module 02 §3](02_remote_work_evidence.md).

**Q10 ★ What happens if your power or internet fails?**
Hotspot in under a minute, battery for X hours, and — the half people forget — immediate communication with an ETA rather than a silent absence.

**Q11. How do you stay productive without someone watching?**
Structure plus record, not personality. Fixed hours, visible in-progress list, and the 27-month Upwork engagement as evidence that it works. Offer the referees.

**Q12. How do you communicate across time zones?**
Written updates before anyone asks — what moved, what's stuck, what I need. The failure mode of remote work is someone quietly blocked for two days, not a missed meeting.

**Q13. What hours can you commit?**
A specific window in Eastern time that you will actually hold. Flexible at the edges for a standing meeting, scheduled rather than ad hoc.

**Q14. Have you worked with US clients before?**
> "Yes — Tekkod is US-based, and the HANWASH engagement through Upwork was with a US team as well. So the working norms are familiar: the meeting culture, the directness, the expectation that you flag a problem early rather than presenting it solved."

**Q15. Is your timezone an issue?**
It is the opposite and you should say so plainly.
> "It's actually one of the better parts. Haiti is Eastern time, so I'm on the same clock as most US clients — full overlap with the working day, no night shift, no waiting until tomorrow for a reply."

**Q16. Do you work from home or a co-working space? Is it quiet?**
Answer concretely — a room, a door, what's behind you on camera. If your background is not presentable, fix it before the call rather than describing it.

## Group 3 — Role fit and positioning

**Q17 ★ You said data analyst or developer — which is it?**
[Module 03 §6](03_dual_track_positioning.md). Give a preference with a reason, keep the door open, and bind the level: *"open on the title, not on the level."*

**Q18. What kind of company do you want to work with?**
> "Honestly, size matters less to me than whether there's a real data problem. I'd rather be at a small company where the reporting is broken and someone needs it fixed than at a large one maintaining something that already works."

**Q19. What's your strongest technical skill?**
> "Making data trustworthy. That sounds less impressive than a tool name, but it's the thing I've been paid for repeatedly — building the checks and the reconciliation so that when a number goes in front of a decision-maker it's right, and when two systems disagree there's a process that finds out why. The tools underneath it are Python, SQL and Power BI."

**Q20. What's your weakest area?**
Name something real, then show the work you are doing on it. Never "I work too hard."
> "Tableau. My dashboard work has been Power BI — I'd call myself expert there — plus Looker Studio, and Tableau keeps coming up in the roles I'm looking at. So I've been working through it over the last few weeks. The concepts transfer more than I expected; a FIXED LOD expression is doing what CALCULATE with ALLEXCEPT does in DAX, which is already how I think. It's the interface I'm still slow in."

**Q21. Do you know Tableau / Power BI / Excel / SQL / Python?**
Answer each with a level and an example, not a yes. Expert in Power BI with the Caris weekly KPI dashboards as proof. SQL daily, including the query optimisation at Tekkod. Python for ETL and reporting automation, forty percent off the manual cycle at Caris. Tableau in progress and say so.

**Q22 ★ Your background is mostly NGO and humanitarian — this is commercial work.**
[Module 03 §6](03_dual_track_positioning.md). The rigour transfers; the vocabulary is what you have been closing. Do not be defensive — donor-funded reporting is audited harder than most commercial reporting.

**Q23. Why do your job dates overlap?**
Ten seconds, plainly, then move on. Consulting engagements in parallel is normal in the Haitian market; Caris was the main programme role while Tekkod and HANWASH ran as contracts. Full map in [`first_step/04 §5`](../first_step/04_positioning_and_mock_interview.md).

**Q24. Are you comfortable being the only technical person on a client's team?**
> "Yes, and it's most of what I've done — on the HANWASH work I was the person who owned the data system, so there was nobody to escalate to. What I'd want in that situation is a clear owner on the client side for decisions, because the thing that stalls a solo technical engagement isn't the technical work, it's waiting on someone to decide what the metric should mean."

**Q25. Would you consider a general virtual assistant or admin role?**
Decline warmly and early. See [module 03 §3](03_dual_track_positioning.md).

## Group 4 — Behavioural

Have one specific story per question. Ninety seconds each, spoken. Situation briefly, what you did in detail, outcome with a number where one exists.

**Q26 ★ Tell me about a difficult problem you solved.**
The Tekkod query optimisation — thirty-six seconds to under half a second. Say what was slow, how you found the cause, what you changed, and what it meant for the person using the system. This is the strongest technical anecdote you own and it needs no translation.

**Q27. Tell me about a mistake you made.**
Pick a real one. Structure: what happened, how you found out, what you did in the first hour, what you changed so it could not recur. The last part is what they are listening for. Data quality work at Caris is fertile ground.

**Q28. Tell me about a time you had to work with unclear requirements.**
Indicator and ITT definition work — someone asks for "a dashboard," and the actual job is agreeing what each number means before anything is built.

**Q29. Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague or client.**
Low drama, resolved by making something concrete. Avoid any story where the resolution is that you were right.

**Q30. Tell me about a time you taught or trained someone.**
Field team training at Caris, HANWASH and Anseye Pou Ayiti, plus the technical guides you wrote. The point to land: you wrote documentation so the capability outlasted you.

**Q31. Tell me about a deadline you missed, or nearly missed.**
Honest, with what you did about it — early warning, renegotiated scope, no silent slippage.

**Q32. How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?**
> "I ask what breaks if it's late, which usually sorts it quickly — a broken report that a decision depends on this week beats a nice-to-have improvement. And I tell whoever's waiting on the thing I'm deprioritising, because most of the pain of a delay is the surprise rather than the delay."

**Q33. How do you handle feedback?**
Short. One example of feedback you acted on. Do not philosophise.

## Group 5 — Practicalities

**Q34. When can you start?**
Have a real date. If you need to give notice at Tekkod, say so — a candidate who leaves an employer cleanly is a candidate who will leave the client cleanly.

**Q35. Full time or part time?**
Say which, and be consistent with the hours you committed to in Q13.

**Q36 ★ What are your salary expectations?**
[Module 04 §4](04_rate_and_engagement_terms.md). Deflect first — *"what band are your technical requisitions at?"* — then stop talking. If forced, give your target with a justification attached, then hand the turn back with a question.

**Q37. Are you comfortable with a contractor arrangement, no benefits?**
> "Yes — most of my work has been contract or consulting, so I'm used to managing my own tax and time off. I'd just want the terms written down: what the payment method and frequency are, and what notice looks like on both sides."

**Q38. Do you have a passport / ID / can you sign a contract?**
Straightforward yes. Do not send documents before there is a signed agreement and a named entity.

**Q39. Can you provide references?**
Yes, immediately, with names ready. See the referee table in [module 02 §6](02_remote_work_evidence.md).

**Q40. Would you take an English or aptitude assessment?**
Yes, cheerfully, and ask when so you can be ready. See [module 00 §2](00_stage_map_and_plan.md).

**Q41. How do you use AI tools?**
The verbatim answer in [`first_step/04 §6`](../first_step/04_positioning_and_mock_interview.md) is strong and applies unchanged — never paste real client data into a prompt, work with schemas and synthetic examples, treat output as a draft to verify. Keep the line about SQL that runs perfectly and answers the wrong question; it is the sentence that proves you have actually used these tools on real work.

## Group 6 — The six questions you must ask them

Asking questions is not a courtesy at the end. Two of these determine whether you can prepare for stage three at all, so get them in even if the call is running short.

**Q42 ★ What roles are you matching me to, and what's the band?**
> "What kinds of requisitions do you have open that you'd see me fitting, and what band are those at?"

This is the most valuable question in the call. Their answer tells you which track to prepare, roughly what the client is like, and what the money looks like — all three unknowns, in one question.

**Q43 ★ Do you place candidates based in Haiti?**
> "Can I ask something practical — do you currently place candidates based in Haiti, and are there any payment or compliance constraints there I should know about?"

Ask it plainly. If the answer is no, you have lost one call instead of three weeks. If yes, the largest unknown in the process is gone.

**Q44. What does stage three look like?**
> "If this goes forward, what's the third interview like — is it with the client directly, and is it usually technical? Anything I should prepare?"

Recruiters often answer this generously, and sometimes tell you exactly what the client asks.

**Q45. What's the timeline, and what happens between now and then?**
> "What's the usual timing from here? And is there anything in between — an assessment, a profile or video, references — that I should get done now rather than waiting?"

Getting the admin done early is free speed, and speed matters to them.

**Q46. What makes a placement work or fail here?**
> "From your side, what separates the placements that go really well from the ones that don't work out?"

Their answer tells you exactly what they screen for — often more candidly than you would expect, and it is useful for stage three.

**Q47. What are the engagement terms?**
The list in [module 04 §5](04_rate_and_engagement_terms.md). You do not need all of it in one call; payment method to Haiti, hours, guarantee period and any exclusivity clause are the four that matter most.
