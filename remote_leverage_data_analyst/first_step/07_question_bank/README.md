# Question bank — Remote Leverage, Data Analyst

*Interview: Monday 3 August 2026, in English. One hundred and twenty-six questions with answers written the way you would say them, plus ten worked case scenarios.*

---

## What this folder is for, and how it differs from the modules

The eight files above this folder teach you the material. Module 01 explains what a funnel is and why censoring breaks a fill-rate trend. Module 03 teaches level-of-detail expressions. They are built for learning, which means the knowledge is organised by *topic* rather than by *the question that will be asked*.

That is the wrong shape for the last forty-eight hours. On Monday nobody will ask you to explain censoring — they will ask *"why did our fill rate drop last month?"*, and you will have about four seconds to recognise that the honest answer is "it probably didn't". This folder inverts the organisation: the question comes first, and the answer sits directly underneath it in complete sentences, at speaking length.

Nothing here invents experience. Every claim traces to `../../../curiculum-vitae-and-letter/specific_situation/remoteleverage_data_analyst/Alexandro_Disla_Resume_RemoteLeverage_EN.md`, and every number was re-executed against `../practice/agency.duckdb` before it was written down. Where an answer depends on a figure, the figure is one you can defend.

## How to use it, which matters more than the content

**Do not read this folder.** Reading it feels productive and produces almost nothing, because recognition is not retrieval. You will finish a file thinking *"yes, I know that"*, walk into the call, and discover that knowing an answer when you can see it and producing it from silence are different skills.

Work from [file 08](08_quiz_no_answers.md) instead. It holds every question with the answers stripped out and the numbering preserved. Read a question, answer it **out loud, at full length, without looking**, and only then open the corresponding file to compare. The gap between what you said and what is written is your actual revision list — and it will be much shorter than the full bank, which is the point.

Three passes is enough if you do them properly. The first pass will be slow and embarrassing; that is the pass doing the work. The second, on Sunday, should feel noticeably easier. The third is only the questions you got wrong twice.

**Answer out loud, in English, every time.** Every other document in this workspace is in French, and your risk on Monday is not knowledge, it is fluency under pressure in a language you have not been rehearsing in. Silent reading trains neither.

## The five questions that decide the interview

If time collapses and you can only prepare five, prepare these. They are the ones where a weak answer is not recoverable and a strong answer is remembered.

**Q1 — Tell me about yourself.** It sets the frame for everything after it. Say "data analyst" in the first five words.

**Q91 — What's your experience with Tableau?** The gap is real, it is the first line of their job description, and the answer is a script, not an improvisation.

**Q47 — Cost per lead or cost per acquisition, and which would you optimise for?** The one question where you can demonstrate commercial instinct rather than claim it.

**Q35 — Tell me about a time you found an error in your own analysis.** The behavioural question that separates people who have shipped analysis from people who have taken courses.

**C6 — What would you do in your first thirty days?** Almost certain to be asked, and your reconciliation answer is genuinely better than what most candidates will say.

## A note on the numbers

Every figure quoted in these answers comes from `../practice/agency.duckdb`, the simulated placement agency in `../practice/`. It is realistic, not real, and the phrase that keeps you honest is **"in the dataset I've been practising on…"**. Say it before the number, every time, without exception. Quoting a practice figure as though it were their data would be the single fastest way to lose the room, and the prefix costs you a second and a half.
