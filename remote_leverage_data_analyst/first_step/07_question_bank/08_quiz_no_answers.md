---

## Why this file exists

Reading an answer and thinking *"yes, I know that"* is recognition, and recognition is not retrieval. On Monday nobody will show you the answer first. The gap between knowing something when you can see it and producing it from silence is exactly the gap that makes people freeze in interviews, and the only way to close it is to practise the harder version.

So: read a question, answer it **out loud, at full length, in English, without looking**. Then open the linked file and compare. The difference between what you said and what is written is your revision list — and it will be far shorter than the full bank, which is the entire point of doing it this way.

## How to run a pass

Three passes is enough if you do them properly.

**Pass one, Saturday.** Slow and uncomfortable. Mark every question you could not answer cleanly. Do not look anything up mid-pass — finish the section first, then check, because breaking to look things up turns retrieval practice back into reading.

**Pass two, Sunday morning.** Only the marked ones. This pass should feel noticeably easier, and where it does not, that question needs a different fix — usually it means you have not decided what your answer is, rather than that you have forgotten it.

**Pass three, Sunday evening.** Only what you missed twice. If that list is longer than about ten questions, stop working on breadth and work on those ten.

Record yourself on the five decisive ones — **Q1, Q91, Q47, Q35 and C6** — and play them back. You will hear the hesitations immediately, and hearing them once is worth ten silent rereads.

## Timing yourself

Rough targets, because length is part of the answer. Profile questions: thirty to sixty seconds, except Q1 which is ninety. Behavioural: sixty to ninety seconds, with the bulk of it on what you *did*. Technical: thirty to sixty seconds — a two-minute answer to "what's the difference between `WHERE` and `HAVING`" is a worse answer, not a fuller one. Cases: two to three minutes, structured out loud.

If you routinely run long, the fix is not to talk faster. It is to state the conclusion first and let them ask for the detail.
